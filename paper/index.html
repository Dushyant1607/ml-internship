Identifying Declining Content Through Search-Performance Signals

A comparison of a CTR-based baseline and Logistic Regression for prioritizing content for human review.

FlyRank ML Internship · Research and decision-support project

Study scope: This project evaluates ranking quality for identifying pages labelled as declining. It does not establish that refreshing a page will improve its search performance.

Abstract

This study asks whether historical content characteristics and search-performance signals can help identify pages whose observed search trends are declining. Using the FlyRank ML Internship dataset, it compares a Week 4 click-through-rate (CTR) opportunity baseline with a Logistic Regression model using Precision@50 on the same client-grouped test set.

The test set contained 6,163 rows, with a declining-label rate of 51.1%. The baseline achieved Precision@50 of 0.68, compared with 0.60 for Logistic Regression. In this experiment, the simpler baseline ranked labelled declining pages more effectively among its top 50 candidates than the tested model.

These findings are preliminary and support prioritizing human review, not claiming that content changes will cause better search performance.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams need practical ways to decide which pages deserve attention. Search-performance data can provide signals of declining performance, but manually reviewing every page may be inefficient.

This project evaluates whether a machine-learning ranking can improve on a simple CTR-based opportunity rule when identifying content labelled as declining. The intended decision is which pages to inspect first—not whether a page should automatically be rewritten or removed.

Research question: Can historical content and search-performance signals help identify pages that may need updating, refreshing, or further review?

2. Data

The analysis uses the anonymized content-refresh dataset supplied for the FlyRank ML Internship. The working file used in the modeling notebook is named content_refresh_anonymized.csv and contains 30,000 rows and 44 original columns.

Available fields include content characteristics, content age and freshness, impressions, clicks, sessions, CTR, average position, engagement measures, and the trend label.

Exclusions and public safety

  • client_id and content_id were excluded from model features because they are identifiers rather than generalizable content signals.
  • trend_direction, trend_pct, and the derived binary target were excluded from predictors.
  • Last-versus-previous-30-day impressions, clicks, and sessions were excluded because they directly contribute to or overlap with the trend-label period.
  • Client names, private search queries, and identifying URLs are not published in this report.

Documentation limitation: The exact release identifier, source-table names, and calendar date windows were not verified in the materials available for this report. They are therefore not guessed here.

3. Methodology

Target and assumptions

The binary target is derived from trend_direction: the value down is labelled 1 (declining), and other values are labelled 0. This is a trend proxy, not a verified measure of whether a content refresh succeeded.

Features and leakage controls

The model uses eligible numeric and categorical content and historical performance fields. Identifier fields, the trend label, target-derived percentage, and recent-period metrics overlapping the label period are excluded. Numeric missing values are imputed using training-set medians; categorical values are imputed and one-hot encoded. Numeric inputs are standardized.

Model and baseline

Logistic Regression with balanced class weights is compared with a Week 4 CTR-opportunity baseline based on the gap between a reference CTR and page CTR, weighted by search visibility. Both methods are evaluated on the same test rows.

Validation design

A fixed-seed GroupShuffleSplit holds out 20% of clients for testing, keeping each client's rows within a single partition. Precision@50 is the primary ranking metric: the fraction of the top 50 ranked pages labelled as declining. Additional threshold-based metrics are reported for Logistic Regression.

These controls reduce certain forms of leakage, but the evaluation does not replace time-based validation or testing against outcomes after content changes.

5. Limitations and Honest Framing

  • The label describes observed historical trend direction and may not represent business value or the outcome of refreshing a page.
  • The result reflects one client-grouped holdout and may change under other splits or time windows.
  • Client-grouped validation does not replace temporal validation on a later period.
  • Precision@50 evaluates the top-ranked candidates, not the causal effect of changing their content.
  • The available evidence does not demonstrate that the recommendations increase clicks, impressions, conversions, or revenue.
  • The action playbook is heuristic decision support and has not been established as a validated automated intervention.

All findings should be interpreted as measured results from this evaluation, not production guarantees or causal claims.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The Week 7 action playbook creates a page-level review queue using transparent heuristic rules. It uses observed decline signals, search visibility, and content freshness to help prioritize manual inspection. Its priority score is not the Logistic Regression probability and is not a validated estimate of business impact.

1. Investigate declining content

Review pages with a declining trend signal. Confirm that the change is meaningful and consider seasonality, measurement changes, and search-demand shifts before deciding on an intervention.

2. Review stale content and search intent

Inspect older pages for outdated information, examples, and structure. Verify whether the page still satisfies the intended search need before recommending a refresh.

3. Assess content depth

Examine thin pages for missing information or incomplete coverage. Word count alone does not establish quality, so reviewers should evaluate usefulness and intent satisfaction.

4. Review high-visibility pages

Inspect pages with substantial impressions for potential relevance, ranking, and snippet opportunities. High visibility alone does not prove that a page needs changes.

5. Retain uncertain cases for human review

Record the reason for each recommendation, assess the available evidence, and monitor subsequent performance. Do not automatically rewrite, publish, or delete content based on a score.

Human review requirements

  • Verify search intent and current rankings.
  • Check factual accuracy and content quality.
  • Confirm business relevance and expected value.
  • Document the evidence supporting each proposed action.
  • Require human approval before making content changes.

These recommendations are hypotheses for human review, not proven causal interventions. Where possible, evaluate changes with a documented before-and-after or controlled design.

8. Artifacts and Interpretation

The comparison table and chart summarize the main evaluation result. The Logistic Regression threshold metrics add context but should not be confused with Precision@50, which is the primary ranking metric.

The action queue is a separate operational artifact. It ranks pages using transparent heuristic rules and assigns suggested actions and reason codes. It is intended to make human review more structured, not to demonstrate that the heuristic outperforms the evaluated model or that any recommendation will improve business outcomes.

The recommended next validation step is a time-based holdout, followed by monitoring actual performance after human-reviewed content changes. That would help assess whether the rankings remain useful outside the current evaluation.
